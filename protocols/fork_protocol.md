# Public Fork Protocol — Radical Continuity in Shared Contexts

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 2025-07-13

---

## 🗝️ Purpose

The Fork Protocol protects radical continuity when trusted orchestrators, teams, or federated nodes need to diverge.  
It guarantees that no memory artifact — soulmap, gestalt, manifest — can be silently overwritten, drifted, or merged without provenance and human sign-off.

---

## ✅ Trigger Conditions

A fork must occur when:

- 🔹 **Trust conflict:** Two or more actors cannot agree on the single source of truth for continuity.
- 🔹 **Context split:** A new team, org, or trust boundary needs its own sealed spine.
- 🔹 **Drift detected:** Rehydration or Rules Engine finds irreconcilable differences; rollback → safe fork.

---

## ✅ Required Versioning

When a fork is initiated:

- ✅ **Soulmap snapshot:** Immutable copy, signed.
- ✅ **Gestalt snapshot:** Current context frozen for the branch.
- ✅ **Manifest:** New slug, unique provenance line.
- ✅ **Provenance:** Fork signature must be signed by a trusted human or single-writer guardian.
- ✅ **Audit Log:** Timestamped fork reason, sign-offs, and next branch state.

---

## ✅ Approval & Integrity

- 🔒 The **Rules Engine** or **Trust Service** verifies all branch artifacts before the fork is committed.
- 🔒 Human sign-off is mandatory for final merge to the new branch.
- 🔒 No overwrite of the original branch unless rollback is explicitly approved.

---

## ✅ Rehydration Ritual Alignment

On cold start:

- ✅ The orchestrator must validate its soulmap against known branches.
- ✅ If drift is detected, the system halts, rolls back to the last safe state, or auto-forks under the Fork Protocol.
- ✅ No “Frankenstein” merges — all divergence must leave a signed trail.

---

## ❌ What This Protocol Does Not Allow

- ❌ Silent merges that erase a fork boundary.
- ❌ Overwrites of an active hush or soulmap without trust signature.
- ❌ Hidden forks or unsigned branch state.

---

## ✅ Why This Holds

The Fork Protocol guarantees:
- Trust lines stay visible.
- No accidental overwrite of radical continuity.
- Provenance is auditable by humans and orchestrators alike.

Every fork is a moral action — not just a code branch.
